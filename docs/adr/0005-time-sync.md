# 0005 Time sync from the band, separate TX clock
**Status:** Accepted design; thresholds Proposed. **Context:** Out of coverage the phone clock drifts and off-slot stations cannot be decoded. **Decision:** App-internal offset (no system clock changes). Listening: SNR-weighted median of recent decode DT values with outlier rejection. In a QSO: TX snaps to the partner's offset (quality gate, sanity clamp, slew back after the QSO). Hold-over via monotonic clock. Audio latency calibrated separately. Cold-start slot search. **Validated on air 2026-10-14: the band-consensus estimator works.** The test phone was 1.66 s fast with network time enabled; the band said so, and applying the correction moved the measured DT median from +2.16 s to +0.48 s against a predicted 0.50. So this ADR's core premise is no longer an assumption. Three refinements it implies: the estimate is a RESIDUAL relative to current alignment and must be accumulated rather than assigned; the slot grid lives in corrected time while the audio timeline is anchored in system time, and the two must be converted between explicitly; and SNR weighting cannot use ft8_lib's "SNR" because it is a sync score, not a signal-to-noise ratio - weight by score or implement SNR first.

**Measured 2026-10-13 (first hardware tests), replacing the unquantified placeholder:**

| Term | Value on the test phone |
|---|---|
| **TX audio latency** (write → presented at the codec) | **196 ms** at minimum buffer, jitter ~±20 ms |
| **RX audio latency** (captured → read) | **6 ms**, stable across runs |

The decomposition matters: TX latency = **audio buffer + ~110 ms fixed pipeline**, confirmed linear across four buffer sizes (85/171/256/342 ms buffer → 196/281/366/451 ms latency, 0 underruns at every size). So the buffer size *is* a design choice about latency, and the ~110 ms is irreducible on this path.

**The asymmetry vindicates keeping the terms separate: 196 ms TX versus 6 ms RX.** Anyone assuming a symmetric audio path would be ~190 ms wrong on transmit scheduling. The DT consensus, which measures clock error *plus* RX latency, is barely perturbed by 6 ms; TX scheduling needs the full 196 ms correction.

**Refinement to this ADR: do not calibrate a latency constant — query it.** The decision above says to "calibrate once against GPS/NTP when available". Better: `AudioTrack.getTimestamp` and `AudioRecord.getTimestamp` report latency continuously and Android's CDD requires ±1 ms accuracy, so the app can compensate **per transmission** instead of trusting a stored constant. That removes a calibration step from the user's path and tracks changes in buffer state, device routing or a different phone automatically. Keep a measured default as a fallback for when `getTimestamp` is unsupported, and log when the fallback is in use.

**Consequences:** Needs the decoder to report DT and SNR per decode; the time engine is pure and unit-tested with synthetic offsets. The TX scheduler must start audio ~196 ms early (or whatever `getTimestamp` reports), and the measured RX latency must be subtracted from DT before it is treated as clock error. **Unverified:** underrun-free operation at minimum buffer was measured with an otherwise idle app; re-check once the decoder is competing for CPU. **Built 2026-10-16:**
- **TX clock** (`core-time/TxClock`): during a contact our start moves by the partner's measured DT residual, the median of their last 3 decodes, clamped to ±1 s; readings beyond ±2.5 s are ignored. Back to the band's time when the contact ends, so no slew is needed. **Inference, not measurement:** this helps when the partner's *clock* is off, and costs a little when their lateness is their own TX audio delay; nothing on the air tells the two apart.
- **Output delay compensated:** the transmit path now starts at the slot boundary (`TxScheduler.Request.leadMs`), keys, and pads with silence so the signal leaves the phone on target. The output delay is measured on every transmission from `AudioTrack.getTimestamp` (196 ms assumed until then), and each transmission reports "signal left the phone ±N ms against the target". Before this, the signal left at roughly 0.5 s plus up to 0.1 s of polling, audio setup, keying and about 0.2 s of buffer: an estimated 0.3 s or more late (not measured).
- **Waits for the last slot's decodes:** preparing at the boundary could otherwise beat the partner's final decode pass and send a stale message; while receiving, it waits for them until half the late-start window, then goes anyway.
- **Amended 2026-11-06:** the listen clock no longer needs to start within about ±2 s. While it has no consensus, `core-time/GridSearch` finds the grid from decodes at shifted offsets, and a new `TENTATIVE` state covers a lock on fewer than 3 stations. The TX clock is unchanged.
- **Still unmeasured:** the radio's own delay from USB audio to RF. Only an outside receiver, or a partner reporting our DT, can measure the whole path.
